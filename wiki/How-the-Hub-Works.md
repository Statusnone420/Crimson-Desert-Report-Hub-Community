# How the Hub works

The Hub keeps sources visible and distinguishes reporting from signals and context.

## Reporting and coverage

Original articles include sources. Selected official, press, and creator coverage links back to its publisher and shows its publication date. External selections receive editorial review before publication.

Automatic patch rundowns use retained official fix excerpts and link to the original notes. Original reporting takes priority when both formats cover the same patch version. An official fix claim describes the developer's statement; player check-ins can show different results.

## The player record

Approved historical written-report excerpts are moderated evidence. Anonymous check-ins are signals about an issue on a specific patch. They do not establish identity, consensus, or a result for every player.

The Hub does not treat silence as a fix. A new patch starts a fresh check-in tally, and older responses remain tied to their patch.

## Source radar and platform trends

Source radar links are leads for further reading. Finding a link does not turn it into a player report or a confirmed problem.

Review and streaming trends describe activity on their source platforms. They do not measure every player and do not prove that a particular issue exists. Missing data stays missing instead of becoming invented activity.

Read the website's [Method page](https://crimsonreporthub.com/about) for more context, or [send a correction](https://github.com/Statusnone420/Crimson-Desert-Report-Hub-Community/issues/new?template=correction.yml) if something needs attention.
